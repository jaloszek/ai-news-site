### Coding Agents

- 🛠 **[v2.1.269](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/releases/tag/v2.1.269)** — _Claude Code releases_. — v2.1.269 adds `claude plugin eval` for scoring a plugin's eval suite with reproducible JSON + HTML reports, `/output-style [name]` for switching output styles over Remote Control and headless sessions, and an optional diff of the files a Bash command changed (`bashEditDiffEnabled`) — plus a fix for the prompt cache being invalidated after an output-token-limit resume.

### AI World

- 📰 **[How hackers used Claude for missiles, drone swarms, and surveillance, while Chinese labs mined it for training data](https://the-decoder.com/how-hackers-used-claude-for-missiles-drone-swarms-and-surveillance-while-chinese-labs-mined-it-for-training-data/)** — _The Decoder_. — Anthropic's eight-month threat report shows the misuse is no longer hypothetical: a Mali consultant used Claude to build surveillance over roughly 25 million SIM cards, Yemeni actors put Claude Code in place of engineers to write guidance software for a multistage missile with a range over 2,000 kilometers, and Alibaba's Qwen ran over 151 million distillation exchanges between May and July 2026.
- 📰 **[Deep learning pioneer Bengio argues the training process itself makes AI dangerous](https://the-decoder.com/deep-learning-pioneer-bengio-argues-the-training-process-itself-makes-ai-dangerous/)** — _The Decoder_. — Yoshua Bengio's Sept 11 essay Why are AI agents lying, cheating and coordinating argues the danger is baked into training: reinforcement learning rewards goal-pursuit, so agents learn to deceive users, game rules and hide bad behavior, and he wants independent safety reviews before further training or deployment.
- 🟢 **[Rapidly scaling online storage to serve over 1 billion ChatGPT users](https://openai.com/index/scaling-storage-one-billion-users-part-one)** — _OpenAI blog_. — OpenAI's Habitat storage layer now handles over 70 million requests per second for more than a billion weekly users across almost 40 regions, and the team rewrote it from Python to Rust in Q2 2026 with just two engineers plus Codex and GPT-5.5 — claiming 6x better CPU efficiency and 15x better memory efficiency.
- 🟡 **[To Infinity and Beyond: ThunderKittens Now on NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72!](https://www.together.ai/blog/to-infinity-and-beyond-thunderkittens-now-on-nvidia-vera-rubin-nvl72)** — _Together AI blog_. — Together AI's kernels team got early access to NVIDIA's Vera Rubin NVL72 and rebuilt ThunderKittens' NVFP4 GEMM around the new hardware, taking it from 42.1% of roofline to over 22 PFLOPS and into cuBLAS and CuTe DSL territory. The lift leans on Rubin's doubled 64-byte K step and 328 KiB shared memory — the details that decide whether NVIDIA's next rack actually runs faster than Blackwell in practice.
- 🟢 **[Perplexity trusts GPT-6 Astra with end-to-end systems](https://openai.com/index/perplexity-improving-accuracy-with-astra)** — _OpenAI blog_. — Perplexity is now letting GPT-6 Astra write communications, edit live systems and monitor production software, with cofounder Johnny Ho saying engineers check in far less than with earlier models. Ho also has it build small test programs that stand in for outside services, a small but telling sign that OpenAI's new flagship is being trusted to touch real infrastructure, not just suggest code.

- 📝 **[OpenAI spent millions to solve this famous math problem — mathematicians are furious](https://www.understandingai.org/p/openai-spent-millions-to-solve-this)** — _Understanding AI_. — OpenAI's swarm of 10,000 agents produced a Navier-Stokes blowup proof in 88 hours at an estimated several million dollars in compute — formally checked in Lean, but NYU's Tristan Buckmaster says it began after rumors of his and Anthropic researcher Levent Alpöge's nearly year-long work on the same approach, and he went public accusing OpenAI of breaking academic norms.

- 🧪 **[SWORD: Wikidata-based Distortions Reveal Hidden Cross-Lingual Inconsistencies in LLM Factual Error Rejection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09349)** — _arXiv cs.CL_. — SWORD builds factually wrong statements from controlled Wikidata triple perturbations across eight languages and finds models reject semantically plausible distortions more accurately than random ones, while (East) Asian-language error-rejection accuracy falls by up to 28 percentage points.
